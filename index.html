Origin and purpose of RFM

Understand the three behavioural signals before touching the model.

Learn

RFM is a simple and powerful way to read commercial behaviour. Instead of looking only at total sales, it asks three questions: how recently did the customer buy, how often do they buy and how much economic value do they generate?

Recency

How close the customer's latest purchase is to the end of the analysed period.

Frequency

How many distinct orders the customer placed during the period.

Monetary

The total order revenue generated by the customer during the period.

Why it still matters

RFM turns transaction data that most companies already have into decisions people can understand: protect valuable customers, recover cooling relationships, activate recent buyers and avoid spending budget without behavioural evidence.

Classic RFM converts real values into ordered factors, typically from 1 to 5. A customer with a high factor for Recency, Frequency and Monetary value may be represented as 555; a weak customer across all three dimensions may appear as 111.

Simple example

If Customer A bought 12 days ago, placed 8 orders and generated €900, the profile could sit close to 555. If Customer B bought 300 days ago, placed one order and generated €40, the profile could sit close to 111. The point is not to memorise codes; it is to understand the behaviour behind them.

Common error: counting products instead of orders

If one order contains five products, it is still one purchase for Frequency. Counting five product lines as five purchases inflates the customer's frequency. This laboratory consolidates repeated order IDs before the RFM calculation.

Classic RFM is a photograph. Dynamic RFM compares two photographs: the previous period (P1) and the current period (P2). The question is no longer only “where is this customer?” but also “how is this relationship moving?”

Upward movement

The customer moves to a stronger segment. The relationship may need recognition, consolidation or development.

Stability

The customer remains in the same segment. The relationship may need maintenance, automation or observation.

Deterioration

The customer moves to a weaker segment. The relationship may need preventive recovery, a call or an economic incentive.

The strategic advantage

Two customers can end today in the same segment and still require opposite actions. One may be improving while the other is falling. Trajectory changes the decision.

Orders with several product lines

For RFM, one order must count once. If the same order ID appears several times for the same customer, the laboratory consolidates those rows and sums their revenue. If one order ID appears under different customers, validation stops.
